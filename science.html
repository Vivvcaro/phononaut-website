The science

Music is more than music

You have felt it. Neuroscience, music psychology and ancestral practices help explain why: sound reaches the systems in us that shape feeling, memory and safety. Phononaut builds on this research and on Viviana Caro’s PhD into sound, ritual and consciousness.

01 / 05 · You have felt it

A song that gave you chills.

The science

Dopamine and endogenous opioid release

Peak moments in music can activate the brain’s reward system, releasing dopamine and the body’s own opioids, which are linked to pleasure.

02 / 05 · You have felt it

A melody that brought back a forgotten memory.

The science

Prefrontal–hippocampal memory circuits

Music engages circuits linking the prefrontal cortex and the hippocampus, which is why a few notes can bring a memory back in vivid detail.

03 / 05 · You have felt it

A voice that made you feel safe.

The science

Oxytocin and vagal regulation

A warm, steady voice can engage the vagus nerve and oxytocin, systems associated with safety, calm and connection.

04 / 05 · You have felt it

A chord that moved you to tears.

The science

Endogenous opioid release

Deeply moving music can release the body’s own opioids, associated with emotional release and comfort.

05 / 05 · You have felt it

A rhythm that changed your mood in seconds.

The science

Dopamine and noradrenaline release

Rhythm can shift arousal quickly through dopamine and noradrenaline, which shape energy, alertness and mood.
